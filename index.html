Hi! I’m Maksim.

I am a Visiting Assistant Professor in the Department of Mathematics and Statistics at the University of Toledo, which I joined in August 2026.

I received my Ph.D. in mathematics from IUPUI in 2023, advised by Vitaly Tarasov and Alexander Its. Before joining Toledo, I held visiting appointments at the University of Cincinnati and Gettysburg College.

My research lies at the intersection of integrable systems, asymptotic analysis, and mathematical physics. I study structures connecting nonlinear differential equations, random matrices, and representation theory, often using Riemann–Hilbert methods to understand their behavior. My recent work also explores non-intersecting Brownian paths and geometric questions about floating bodies. Beyond mathematics, I enjoy collaborative projects in data science and machine learning.

My background is in physics: I earned both my bachelor’s and master’s degrees at St. Petersburg State University in Russia, where I studied quantum field theory and quantum electrodynamics (QED).

2026Published · Journal of Statistical Physics 193, 93

A Two-HCIZ Gaussian Matrix Model for Non-Intersecting Brownian Bridges

Maksim Kosmakov

Abstract

We construct a unitarily invariant Hermitian matrix ensemble whose fixed-time eigenvalue law coincides with the Karlin–McGregor law for non-intersecting Brownian bridges with arbitrary finite multiplicities at both endpoints. This provides an explicit matrix-ensemble realization of the known mixed-type multiple orthogonal polynomial and Riemann–Hilbert description of the general multi-start/multi-end problem. We then derive several exact finite-n consequences of this construction.

These include a path-space lift as an orbital Hermitian Brownian bridge and a reduction of the partition function to a single compact HCIZ integral with explicit t-dependence. We also compare the one-sided reduction with the Gaussian external-field ensemble, showing that, although the two ensembles are spectrally equivalent, their angular statistics are different. Finally, we derive fixed-time Schwinger–Dyson identities and associated resolvent relations for the dressed ensemble.

2026Preprint · arXiv:2607.08338

Rigidity in the planar Ulam floating body problem with perimetral densities σ = 1/8, 3/8 under central symmetry

Oleg Asipchuk, Maksim Kosmakov, Pavel Zatitskii

Abstract

We prove that the only planar, centrally symmetric, strictly convex body K ⊂ ℝ2 with C1 boundary that floats in equilibrium in every orientation for the perimetral densities σ = 1/8 or σ = 3/8 is a disk.

2026Preprint · arXiv:2606.27594

Reduction of Multiple Orthogonal Polynomials to Standard Orthogonal Polynomials

Roozbeh Gharakhloo, Maksim Kosmakov, Kenta Miyahara

Abstract

In this article, we derive explicit formulae expressing multiple orthogonal polynomials in terms of standard orthogonal polynomials. We treat both the real-line and unit-circle settings: multiple orthogonal polynomials on the real line (MOPRL) are reduced to orthogonal polynomials on the real line (OPRL), while multiple orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle (MOPUC) are reduced to orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle (OPUC).

These formulae also yield corresponding reductions of the Christoffel–Darboux kernels, from the MOPRL kernel to the OPRL kernel and from the MOPUC kernel to the OPUC kernel. In particular, they recover Zinn-Justin’s kernel for the external-source random matrix model [arXiv:cond-mat/9703033] and Baik’s kernel reduction formula in the spiked source model [arXiv:0809.3970].

We also apply our general results to concrete examples: in the real-line setting, we obtain an explicit expression for the multiple Hermite Christoffel–Darboux kernel in terms of classical Hermite polynomials, while in the unit-circle setting, we use arc-indicator weights to exhibit resonance-type zero escape phenomena for type II MOPUC.

2026Published · International Mathematics Research Notices 2026 (18), rnag203 · arXiv:2604.10330

Rigidity in the Planar Ulam Floating Body Problem with perimetral density σ = 1/6

Oleg Asipchuk, Maksim Kosmakov, Pavel Zatitskii

Abstract

We study the two-dimensional Ulam’s floating body problem for convex domains with perimetral density σ = 1/6. Using the framework of Zindler carousels, we reduce the problem to a two-dimensional dynamical system associated with an inscribed equilateral hexagon. Our main result shows that the disk is the only convex domain floating in equilibrium in every position for this perimetral density. This provides a new rigidity result for rational perimetral densities in the convex setting.

2025Published · Nonlinearity 38, 105016

Connection formulae for the radial Toda equations II

Martin A. Guest, Alexander R. Its, Maksim Kosmakov, Kenta Miyahara, Ryosuke Odoi

Abstract

This is a continuation of [arXiv:2309.16550] in which we computed the asymptotics near x = ∞ of all solutions of the radial Toda equation. In this article, we compute the asymptotics near x = ∞ of all solutions of a “partner” equation. The equations are related in the sense that their respective monodromy data constitute connected components of the same “monodromy manifold”.

While all solutions of the radial Toda equation are smooth, those of the partner equation have infinitely many singularities, and this makes the Riemann-Hilbert nonlinear steepest descent method (and the asymptotics of solutions) more involved.

New combinatorial formulae for nested Bethe vectors

2025Published · Nonlinearity 38, 035015

Connection formulae for the radial Toda equations I

Martin A. Guest, Alexander R. Its, Maksim Kosmakov, Kenta Miyahara, Ryosuke Odoi

Abstract

This paper is the first in a forthcoming series of works where the authors study the global asymptotic behavior of the radial solutions of the 2D periodic Toda equation of type An. The principal issue is the connection formulae between the asymptotic parameters describing the behavior of the general solution at zero and infinity.

To reach this goal we are using a fusion of the PDE analysis and the Riemann-Hilbert nonlinear steepest descent method of Deift and Zhou which is applicable to 2D Toda in view of its Lax integrability. A principal technical challenge is the extension of the nonlinear steepest descent analysis to Riemann-Hilbert problems of matrix rank greater than 2. In this paper, we meet this challenge for the case n = 2 (the rank 3 case) and it already captures the principal features of the general n case.

Applied mathematics & finance

2026SSRN

Factor Neutralization and Risk-Budgeted Scaling: Evidence from Long-Short Anomaly Portfolios

Maksim Kosmakov

Abstract

Factor-neutral portfolios lower modeled risk but can also remove expected return. We derive an exact ex post feasibility interval linking return retention, volatility compression, leverage, and a relative-risk budget. Using 212 Open Source Asset Pricing long-short strategies, we test a controlled 133-strategy panel across nine nonoverlapping five-year targets. Projection onto the Fama-French five factors plus momentum raises the average target Sharpe ratio from 2.25 to 3.57. Applying the exploratory fixed scale c = 1.50 increases average annualized return from 6.62% to 9.93%, versus 8.73% for the raw portfolio scaled to c = 1.10, while meeting the risk budget in every target. U.S. and international Kenneth French portfolios show the failure case: neutralization is ineffective when projection removes most of the expected-return sleeve. The results identify residual mean retention as the central condition for deployable neutral scaling.

2026SSRN

Capital-Level Factor Neutrality with Projected Partial Rebalancing

Maksim Kosmakov

Abstract

This paper studies a portfolio-construction method that imposes factor neutrality directly on implemented capital weights, rather than only residualising the alpha signal before optimisation. At each rebalance, portfolios are represented in coordinates inside the monitored neutral subspace, making the final traded portfolio factor-neutral by construction. The method is equivalent to equality-constrained Markowitz optimisation and gives finite-sample leakage bounds when exposure definitions differ.

The construction is extended to projected partial rebalancing, where the previous holding is first projected into the current neutral subspace and then moved part way toward the full neutral target. Walk-forward tests across six research universes show numerical-level exposure leakage for neutral-coordinate portfolios and lower turnover for projected partial variants.
